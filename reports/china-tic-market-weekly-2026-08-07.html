3. Executive Summary

  • Competitors are productizing digital platforms and direct channels, not just selling tests: Amazon DV/CFT, InterLink eFiling, LRQA EiQ, SGS Compass, SGS FoodNexus, Inspectorio SCNI, Pivot88, SafetyCulture, Checkfirst, and IFS all convert assurance evidence into software or platform workflows.
  • Platform-direct compliance is now a front-line e-commerce sales argument: Intertek, BV, TÜV Rheinland, UL, SGS, Eurofins, Cotecna, and CQC-related sources all point to direct provider-to-Amazon workflows for selected categories and marketplaces.
  • AI is most credible where it reduces evidence friction: supplier incident monitoring, food risk prediction, AI audit document review, field-photo verification, inspection risk scoring, report summaries, and lab workflow automation are concrete use cases.
  • QC SaaS competitors are converging on QIMA's operational workflow: Inspectorio, TradeBeyond/Pivot88, SafetyCulture, The Inspection Company, and Checkfirst compete for inspection booking, mobile evidence capture, risk dashboards, and supplier data budgets.

4. QIMA Implications

Amazon DV / CFT / CPSC eFiling

  • Maintain a live category battlecard by marketplace: toys, furniture/CSU, lithium batteries, power banks/portable power supplies, laser products, e-mobility, and electronics.
  • Package "platform-direct compliance" carefully: separate Amazon DV, Amazon Compliance Fast Track, report validation, label/manual review, and CPSC eFiling data support.
  • Sales language should say "TIC/provider submits validated compliance results directly" only where the source confirms it; avoid LIMS/raw-data automation claims without evidence.

ESG / supplier intelligence platforms

  • Position QIMAone as a supplier intelligence layer combining audit history, CAP status, inspection defects, certificate status, risk segmentation, and external incident signals.
  • Benchmark against LRQA EiQ, SGS Compass, Sourcemap, BV Clarity/AITrack, and IFS process checks; these products are selling dashboards, not just assessment days.
  • Use evidence-first differentiation against AI-only mapping: verified supplier data, inspector/auditor evidence, and chain-of-custody documents are QIMA strengths.

OT cyber / AI governance / QC SaaS

  • Explore partner-led OT cyber packages where QIMA lacks implementation depth, mirroring Kiwa x Siemens for IEC 62443/NIS2/CRA.
  • Pilot AI field productivity features: photo completeness checks, report drafting QA, offline evidence capture, and auditor scheduling optimization.
  • For ERP/QMS customers, sell integrations and assurance depth; IFS Cloud-style native QMS can reduce appetite for standalone inspection portals.

6. Market & Regulation Trends (folded appendix)

TIC Council / IAF / accreditation policy signals
  • TIC Council's digitalisation framework supports AI, IoT, digital twins, and digitally collected evidence in assurance services, but stresses independence, impartiality, confidentiality, human-in-the-loop oversight, and human accountability. [S39]
  • TIC Council's 2026 Summit and QI roundtable emphasized smart, machine-readable standards, AI-assisted standards drafting, digital interoperability, and AI literacy training for quality infrastructure practitioners. [S40] [S41]
  • IAF/ILAC have moved into Global Accreditation Cooperation Incorporated (Global ACI) as of 1 January 2026, consolidating the global MRA framework; this matters for future ISO/IEC 42001 and other digital assurance mutual-recognition claims. [S42]
L8 market research references - directional only
  • MarketsandMarkets estimates TIC at USD 254.41B in 2026 and USD 306.13B by 2031 at 3.8% CAGR; source is a public report summary/press-style excerpt, so use only directionally. [S43]
  • Mordor estimates TIC at USD 275.56B in 2026 and USD 345.39B by 2031 at 4.62% CAGR; it highlights ESG, carbon verification, cybersecurity, and digital transformation as growth drivers. [S44]
  • Fortune Business Insights estimates USD 275.39B in 2026 and USD 442.25B by 2034 at 6.10% CAGR; Grand View Research estimates a much larger USD 434.89B in 2026 and USD 555.92B by 2033. These public figures conflict because scope and methodology differ. [S45] [S46]
  • BCG and Deloitte trend materials reinforce the same direction: digitized TIC operations, AI/IoT/cloud-enabled assurance, cybersecurity/data integrity, ESG assurance, and Digital Product Passports are becoming strategic demand pools rather than appendix topics. [S47] [S48]
