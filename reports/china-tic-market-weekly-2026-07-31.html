3. Executive Summary

  • Competitors are productizing digital platforms, not only selling tests: Amazon DV/CFT, CPSC eFiling, SGS Compass, LRQA EiQ, Inspectorio SCNI, Pivot88, Sourcemap, SafetyCulture, Checkfirst and IFS all shift customer value from a PDF report to a workflow, data layer, or SaaS system.
  • Amazon DV is the urgent platform-direct signal: the strongest evidence shows TIC providers directly submit validation results to Amazon for affected categories; seller self-upload is not accepted in those workflows.
  • ESG is becoming data infrastructure: SGS Compass, LRQA EiQ, BV AITrack, Sourcemap, and IFS checks show ESG/CSDDD/CSRD work is being sold as supplier data collection, risk scoring, evidence management, and verification readiness.
  • AI is most credible when tied to specific workflow pain: Paramo risk scoring, Checkfirst scheduling/photo verification, BV AIRI audit acceleration, SGS FoodNexus risk prediction, and CTI data-review automation are clearer than generic AI messaging.

4. QIMA Implications

Amazon DV / platform direct

  • Build a sales one-pager mapping Amazon DV/CFT categories by site, provider workflow, report validity, TRF/MYC step, and QIMA capability gaps.
  • Position QIMA as a compliance-channel operator where direct submission is confirmed; avoid claiming raw data synchronization until integration evidence exists.
  • Bundle Amazon DV with CPSC eFiling readiness for U.S. consumer product importers.

ESG platform / supplier intelligence

  • Package factory audit, CAPA, certificate, document, and external-risk signals into a customer-facing supplier risk view.
  • Use LRQA EiQ and SGS Compass as benchmarks for modules: adverse media, supplier scoring, certificate/test record integration, and CSRD/CSDDD evidence exports.
  • Where QIMA lacks a proprietary module, prepare integration narratives with ERP, PLM, and traceability platforms.

OT cyber / AI governance

  • Explore industrial cyber partnerships that combine implementation partners with independent QIMA-style assessment.
  • Track IEC 62443, NIS2, CRA, RED-DA, and AI Act demand by customer segment rather than treating cyber as generic ISO 27001.
  • Pilot AI-enabled audit productivity in bounded use cases: photo completeness, scheduling, document review, and report drafting.

6. Market & Regulation Trends

TIC Council and IAF / Global ACI: digital assurance needs human oversight and accreditation clarity
  • TIC Council's 2026 digitalisation framework supports use of AI, IoT, digital twins, remote inspection, and continuous assurance, but insists on independence, impartiality, technical competence, confidentiality, human-in-the-loop oversight, explainability, auditability, and data integrity. [S40]
  • TIC Council also urges NIS2 implementation and recognition of ISO/IEC 27001/independent third-party assessment as a way to reduce fragmentation and build trust. [S41]
  • IAF/ILAC transition to Global Accreditation Cooperation / Global ACI is relevant for cross-border acceptance of accredited conformity assessment results. IAF MD 4:2025 also treats AI as part of ICT used in conformity assessment methodology. [S42] [S43]
L8 directional market sizing: use only as appendix context; source methodologies conflict
  • MarketsandMarkets estimates TIC market growth from USD 254.41B in 2026 to USD 306.13B by 2031 at 3.8% CAGR, citing digital reporting, connected labs, automation, remote audits, ESG and traceability demand. This is a report summary / PR-style public extract. [S44]
  • Mordor estimates USD 275.56B in 2026 and USD 345.39B by 2031 at 4.62% CAGR, with digital transformation, ESG, cybersecurity and automation as drivers. Free public summary only. [S45]
  • Fortune Business Insights estimates USD 275.39B in 2026 and USD 442.25B by 2034 at 6.1% CAGR; Grand View Research uses a much larger scope, estimating USD 434.89B in 2026 and USD 555.92B by 2033 at 3.6% CAGR. Treat the range as directional, not comparable. [S46] [S47]
  • BCG's TIC digitalisation work remains useful as transformation context: TIC companies need digital platforms, customer engagement, digitized core operations, and M&A/partnerships to avoid losing workflow ownership to digital-native entrants. [S48]
  • Deloitte's Digital Product Passport and EUDR materials reinforce the customer problem: regulatory compliance increasingly requires structured, accurate, system-integrated product and supply-chain data, not just certificates. [S49]
